# Amazon Keyword Demand Matrix

## Overview

Build a demand-first Amazon listing strategy from structured evidence instead of isolated keyword sorting or lightweight VOC summaries.

Prioritize what each keyword actually means to the buyer, then map that meaning into listing communication.

## Core Workflow

### 1. Inspect and scope the inputs

- Confirm the exact product scope before analyzing keywords.
- Identify the usable inputs:
  - product information such as product name, specs, attributes, brand positioning
  - user-demand mapping such as persona, JTBD, scenarios, workflow, pain points, decision drivers, emotional needs, purchase drivers
  - keyword libraries such as core, traffic, conversion, long-tail, function, attribute, scenario, audience, problem, competitor keywords
  - optional VOC or review summaries that strengthen demand evidence
- If the product file contains multiple product lines or parents, filter to the exact requested scope first.

### 2. Extract product truth

- Summarize what the product actually sells, not what the seller hopes it sells.
- State:
  - product positioning
  - product value
  - core competitiveness
  - biggest difference versus ordinary alternatives
- Use only evidence available in the inputs.

### 3. Summarize demand

- Answer five questions:
  - who is buying
  - why they buy
  - where they use it
  - what they care about most
  - what they fear most
- Prefer explicit evidence from the demand map or VOC.
- If a scenario or persona is plausible but unsupported, mark it as insufficient evidence instead of inferring it as fact.

### 4. Interpret keywords by demand, not by taxonomy alone

- Do not stop at labels like "scenario word" or "attribute word."
- For each keyword, explain:
  - search intent
  - real user task
  - implied scenario
  - implied pain point
  - purchase driver behind the click
- Separate:
  - good-fit keywords
  - weak-fit keywords
  - misleading keywords that may drive wrong traffic or wrong expectations

### 5. Build the matrixes

- Before drafting the final deliverable, read [references/output-structure.md](references/output-structure.md).
- When the user works in Chinese or wants a ready-to-deliver Chinese report, also read [references/chinese-default-template.md](references/chinese-default-template.md).
- Build the required keyword, communication, image, and bullet mappings.
- Make every matrix row useful for downstream listing decisions.

### 6. Close with strategy and gaps

- Translate the matrix into:
  - title strategy
  - A+ strategy
  - SEO coverage recommendations
  - missing-demand analysis
  - optimization priority by `P0`, `P1`, `P2`
- Treat unsupported claims as gaps, not conclusions.

## Non-Negotiables

- Do not fabricate user demand.
- Do not force coverage when the keyword and demand evidence do not match.
- Do not repeat long keyword lists without interpretation.
- Do not write the final title unless the user explicitly asks for it.
- Distinguish observation from inference.
- If evidence is weak, say `证据不足` and explain what is missing.

## Drafting Rules

- Keep the final report structured and auditable.
- Prefer tables for matrix outputs.
- Use Chinese when the user is working in Chinese unless asked otherwise.
- Prefer the Chinese section names and phrasing patterns in [references/chinese-default-template.md](references/chinese-default-template.md) when drafting Chinese deliverables.
- Make the report useful for Title, Bullet, Images, A+, and Backend Search Terms teams at the same time.
- If the user asks for HTML or other deliverables, keep the same chapter structure and matrix logic.
