# Decision Report Structure

Use the sections needed for the user’s decision. For a full ecosystem analysis, use this order.

1. **Executive decision** — market scope, size, entry/expansion judgment, confidence, and the two to four facts that matter most.
2. **Data scope and cleaning** — source coverage, data dates, inclusion/exclusion logic, and residual limitations.
3. **Market map** — brand shares, concentration, price bands, product/technical structure, seller geography, and trend. Use both unit and revenue views.
4. **Competitive ecosystem** — winning models and representative brands/ASINs; summarize each moat and weakness.
5. **Keyword and traffic battlefield** — demand/search, conversion, ranking difficulty, traffic-source, and PPC observations only to the extent those data are actually present.
6. **Gap map** — opportunity candidates scored by demand validation, competition, value capture, differentiation, extendability, and execution. Name non-opportunities and the reason to avoid them.
7. **Entry or portfolio strategy** — positioning, initial SKU(s), pricing logic, launch sequence, and product-line extension path. Keep recommendations conditional on evidence.
8. **Risk and validation plan** — risks, blocked assumptions, experiments, success thresholds, estimated timing, and ownership if requested.
9. **Evidence boundary** — a concise table of what the evidence supports, what is inferred, and what cannot yet be claimed.

## Minimum tables

- Data cleaning audit
- Brand share and concentration
- Price-band structure
- Product-form × key-attribute matrix
- Competitor winning-model comparison
- Opportunity priority matrix
- Assumption-validation backlog

## Writing rules

- State the scope above the executive conclusion so readers do not mistake a keyword market for the entire category.
- Put exact denominators, data periods, and units in table headers or notes.
- Write each recommendation as: recommended action → evidence → trade-off/risk → validation needed.
- Avoid a generic SWOT unless it produces a concrete decision or test.
