# Evidence Rules for Amazon Product Video Strategy

## Evidence labels

Use visible labels in the deliverable:

- **A — direct evidence:** Listing field, manual, actual review, supplied research table, or video that was genuinely viewed.
- **B — synthesis:** recurring pattern in tagged reviews or multiple verified sources; preserve the actual sample and denominator.
- **C — creative/strategic inference:** a reasonable interpretation that could guide testing, not a product fact.
- **D — verify before use:** missing technical detail, performance claim, scene condition, or asset that must be checked before production or publication.

## VOC counting

- Use the number of unique reviews as the denominator, not duplicate tag-chain rows.
- Disclose if tags are rule-based, human-tagged, multi-label, category-level, or mixed with own-product reviews.
- Quote concise representative language and attach ASIN/variation context when available.
- Treat severe failure points as high priority even when their observed rate is low; describe them as high-consequence rather than artificially inflating prevalence.

## Product claims and visual evidence

- A dimension, material, compatibility range, included accessory, or certification must come from the selected child SKU or a clearly parent-wide source.
- Listing adjectives such as “professional,” “effortless,” “perfect,” “durable,” and “safe” are seller claims, not independently verified outcomes.
- A close-up can prove what a surface looks like; it cannot prove electrical resistance, holding force, lifespan, waterproofing, or certification.
- A comparison needs matched conditions, stated materials, and a non-misleading source for each sample.

## PDP video research

- Separate the displayed aggregate count from individually visible cards.
- Record title, creator/account, displayed duration, ownership marker, and PDP URL only when exposed.
- Do not infer a video’s opening, demonstration, subtitles, or quality from its title or thumbnail.
- If playback or the actual asset cannot be reviewed, describe findings as metadata-level signals only.
